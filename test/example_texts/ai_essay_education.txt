In the article Making Mona Lisa Smile, the author discusses the development of the Facial Action Coding System (FACS), a technology capable of detecting human emotions through facial expressions. The author raises the question of whether this technology would be effective in classrooms, where teachers already possess the ability to sense and respond to student emotions effectively. As a 10th-grade student, I believe that the FACS system would not be a valuable tool in the classroom for several reasons.

Firstly, facial expressions are not always reliable indicators of a person's emotions. A person may smile politely while feeling upset, or may maintain a neutral expression while listening carefully. Secondly, using such technology in the classroom raises significant privacy concerns, as students would be subject to constant monitoring. In conclusion, I believe that the Facial Action Coding System is not 100 percent valuable and correct for classroom use.
